What happens after you send an Oldham fire stopping enquiry?

First, we work out what is actually stopping the project moving forward. If you have a compartmentation report, we review the relevant defects and locations. If new M&E work is the issue, clear photos usually show what further detail we need on the wall or floor, cable trays, pipes, ducts or opening dimensions. For structural steel, we will ask for the steel schedule and required fire-resistance period.

You do not have to wait for a perfect information pack. A facilities manager in Royton may only have survey photos. A contractor in Chadderton may have marked-up drawings and a handover date. A warehouse team in Hollinwood may need high-level work planned around access equipment and normal operations.

Tell us the real deadline. If Building Control, a fire-risk assessor, client inspection or ceiling closure is driving the programme, say so at the start. Where the scope is clear we may be able to price from supplied information; where construction or access is uncertain, a site visit can be the sensible next step.

Fire stopping above ceilings & hidden compartmentation defects in Oldham

Some of the most disruptive defects are the ones nobody can see from floor level. New cables can pass through an old seal, batt can be damaged during later M&E work, or an opening can be left incomplete above a suspended ceiling. These issues often appear during a compartmentation survey or late in a refurbishment programme.

If the defect is above a ceiling, send photos before access is closed. Tell us the ceiling height, whether tiles are removable, whether access equipment is already on site and whether the area remains occupied. That information helps us understand both the fire stopping requirement and the practical job.
