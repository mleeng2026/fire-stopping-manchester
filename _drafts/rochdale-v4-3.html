What happens after you send a Rochdale fire stopping enquiry?

First, we work out what is actually holding the project up. If you have a compartmentation report, we review the relevant defects and locations. If new M&E work is the issue, clear photographs normally show what further information is needed on the wall or floor, cable trays, pipes, ducts or opening dimensions. For structural steel, we will ask for the steel schedule and required fire-resistance period.

You do not have to wait for a perfect information pack. A facilities manager in Middleton may only have survey photos. A contractor in Rochdale town centre may have marked-up drawings and a handover date. A warehouse team around Heywood may need high-level work planned around access equipment, loading and normal operations.

Tell us the real deadline. If Building Control, a fire-risk assessor, client inspection or ceiling closure is driving the programme, say so at the start. Where the scope is clear we may be able to price from supplied information; where construction or access is uncertain, a site visit can be the sensible next step.

Fire stopping above ceilings & hidden compartmentation defects in Rochdale

Some defects only become obvious during a compartmentation survey or refurbishment. New cables can pass through an old seal, batt can be damaged by later M&E work, or an opening can be left incomplete above a suspended ceiling.

If the defect is above a ceiling, send photographs before access is closed. Tell us the ceiling height, whether tiles can be removed, whether access equipment is already on site and whether the area remains occupied. That helps us understand both the fire stopping requirement and the practical job.
